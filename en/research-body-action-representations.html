Overview

Visual representations of body parts in autism spectrum disorder

Perceiving body parts such as faces, hands, and feet is important for communication. We examined how body-part information is organized in the brain by comparing adults with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and typically developing (TD) adults (Kurihara et al., 2025, Imaging Neuroscience).

We recorded fMRI activity while participants viewed body-part images, focusing on the lateral occipitotemporal cortex (LOTC). Representational similarity analysis (RSA) allowed us to compare which body parts evoked similar patterns of brain activity (Figure 1).

Here, “representational structure” refers to an organization of information inferred from similarities in response patterns. How that organization is actually used in cognition and behavior requires separate investigation.

In both groups, body parts formed three clusters—limbs, torso, and face parts—with similar representational structures in the left and right LOTC (Figure 2). These findings suggest comparable basic visual representations of body parts in the adults studied. They motivate further investigation into how visual information is interpreted and used in interactions with others.
